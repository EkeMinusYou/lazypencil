# Content pages

Identify the audience and purpose: an article may need a clear reading sequence, a reference page easy navigation, and a promotional page an action to take. Establish a title and sections that support that purpose. Use supplied content and distinguish examples or placeholders from factual claims.

Choose section structure, typography, and spacing from the material. Add a hero, imagery, illustrations, or a call to action when they serve the page's purpose. The optional content-page style offers a spacious reading column and warm palette; other visual directions are valid.

When an image is a style reference, use it to understand hierarchy, density, composition, and imagery without assuming its text or assets belong in the new page. When the user requests faithful reproduction of supplied material, preserve the requested copy and details. Use assets with suitable usage rights and follow explicit brand, language, and accessibility requirements.

Inspect the first viewport and full page. Check reading order, line length, section spacing, and long copy at the target width. Check navigation or calls to action where the page needs them.
